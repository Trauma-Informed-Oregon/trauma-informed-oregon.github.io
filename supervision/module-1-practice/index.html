Module 1 Practice Kit

Trauma, Healing & Identity in Supervision

Use this after Module 1 and before your next supervision session. The focus is identity, power, context, process, and parallel process.

Four anchors

What to keep in view.

Safe enough

Supervision that is non-judgmental, accepting, predictable, and built on trust and clear expectations.

Context

Trauma and healing happen within family, community, culture and society, and systems and institutions. Nobody lives in a vacuum.

Equity

Trauma informed work and equity work belong together. Race, power, privilege, and inequity shape supervision.

Parallel process

What you model in supervision can travel to the people your supervisees serve. Healing and harm both travel.

Questions to return to

Look at the relationship and the process.

1
Identity

Which of my identities shows up most in this supervisory relationship?

2
Comfort and power

When might I be confusing my comfort with what is right? Where do I hold privilege in this role?

3
Feedback

What feedback have I avoided because identity or power made the conversation harder?

4
Inequity

How do I respond when a supervisee names inequity?

Before your next supervision session

Notice one identity
Pick the identity that shows up most with one supervisee and notice how it shapes what you say, hear, and act on.
Check the process
Look for one place where how you did the work matched what you value and one place where it did not.
Find your person
Name one supervisor or peer you can talk with about equity-centered, trauma informed supervision.

Use it in real supervision

A five-minute reset before you meet.

1
Notice

Name the identity, power, or context dynamic most likely to shape this supervision session.

2
Choose

Decide what you want to make more possible in the relationship: clarity, safety enough, accountability, repair, or room for another perspective.

3
Check the process

After the meeting, ask whether how you supervised matched what you say you value.
