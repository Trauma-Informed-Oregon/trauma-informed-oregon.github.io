Module 3 Practice Kit

Proactive Approaches to Navigating Conflict & Tension

Use this to prepare for everyday relational conflict that can be navigated safely and voluntarily. Abuse, violence, harassment, discrimination, threats, or situations requiring formal HR or agency processes are outside the scope of this tool.

Start here

Conflict gives you information.

Identity, history, role, fear, and power change the stakes of conflict. The same situation can carry different risk for different people.

Fear

Supervisees and supervisors may carry fear shaped by role, identity, discrimination, prior authority relationships, and past conflict.

Accountability

Clarity and accountability can coexist with compassion, context, and relationship.

Consent

People cannot be forced into relational conflict work. If they do not want to engage, the minimum goal may be civility and functional coexistence.

Regulation

When people are highly activated, it may be better to pause and create enough space for thinking and participation before trying to process the conflict.

Trauma-Sensitive Conflict Mapping

Build the map before you need it.

Safety

What needs to be true for people to be safe enough to engage?

Relationship and connection

What foundation exists here? What needs protection, clarity, or repair?

Co-regulation

What pacing, support, time, space, or facilitation would help people stay able to participate?

Postvention outcomes

What would repair, accountability, harm reduction, learning, or functional coexistence look like?

Compassionate curiosity

Check the conditions before you push the conversation forward.

1
Acknowledge what is happening

Name the emotion, impact, or tension without diagnosing the person.

2
Get curious with compassion

Ask from genuine interest. Be willing to learn that your first read was incomplete.

3
Move toward joint problem-solving

Decide what can be worked out together and what remains your responsibility as supervisor.

Use the map in this order

Safety
Can people participate without immediate threat or coercion?
Relationship
What does the relationship need before the issue can be worked?
Co-regulation
What support or pacing is needed?
Outcome
What is a realistic next place to get to?

Use it in real supervision

Map before you mediate.

1
Scope

Confirm that this is relational conflict people can safely and voluntarily work on together. Use formal agency or HR processes when the situation calls for them.

2
Map

Look at safety, relationship and connection, co-regulation, and the outcome people are actually trying to reach.

3
Open

Choose a first sentence that names what is happening without diagnosing, cornering, or forcing disclosure.

4
Follow through

Decide what needs repair, accountability, another conversation, or a different process after the immediate tension settles.
